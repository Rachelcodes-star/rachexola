Understand the science

What makes a mineral?

A mineral is generally a naturally occurring, inorganic solid with an ordered internal structure and a characteristic chemical composition. Rocks are made of one or more minerals, while ores are materials from which useful substances can be extracted economically.

🧪

Chemical composition

Minerals contain particular chemical elements arranged in specific proportions. A formula such as SiO₂ represents quartz.

💎

Hardness and cleavage

The Mohs scale compares scratch resistance from talc at 1 to diamond at 10. Cleavage describes how a mineral breaks along planes of weakness.

🔬

Crystal structure

Atoms are arranged in repeating patterns. Crystal form, lustre, streak, density and magnetism help geologists identify specimens.

🌋

How minerals form

Minerals can crystallise from cooling magma, precipitate from water, form through heat and pressure, or develop during weathering.

🪨

Rocks and the rock cycle

Igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks form and change through geological processes over very long periods.

🧭

Mineral exploration

Geologists study maps, outcrops, geophysical measurements and carefully collected samples to understand possible deposits.

Learning note: Mohs hardness is a relative scratch test, not a measure of toughness. A hard mineral can still be brittle. Values can vary slightly with specimen and testing conditions.
From the ground to everyday life

Mining and mineral processing

Mining is a sequence of technical, environmental and social decisions — not simply digging rocks out of the ground.

🗺️

1. Exploration

Geological mapping, sampling and surveys help determine whether a mineral deposit may exist and how it is distributed.

⛏️

2. Extraction

Surface mines and underground mines are selected according to deposit depth, geology, safety, economics and environmental conditions.

⚙️

3. Processing

Crushing, grinding, sorting and separation can concentrate valuable minerals. The exact process depends on the ore.

🚚

4. Refining and supply

Concentrates may be smelted, refined or chemically processed before materials enter manufacturing supply chains.

♻️

5. Recycling

Recovering metals from electronics, batteries, vehicles and scrap can reduce demand for newly mined materials.

🌱

6. Mine closure

Closure planning, land rehabilitation, water monitoring and community engagement should be considered throughout a mine's life.

Local geology · Global connections

Minerals and mining in Rwanda

Rwanda has a mining sector associated with tin, tantalum and tungsten minerals, as well as gold and other mineral resources. The country is part of the Central African region's wider geological systems.

Tin · Cassiterite

Cassiterite is the principal ore of tin. Tin is used in solder, coatings and alloys.

Tantalum · Tantalite

Tantalum is used in electronic capacitors and specialised alloys. Tantalite is one mineral source.

Tungsten · Wolframite

Tungsten is valued for its very high melting point and is used in hard materials and industrial tools.

Responsible sourcing: Mineral supply chains should support traceability, safe working conditions, environmental protection and benefits for local communities. Production rankings and regulations change, so consult current official sources for the latest figures.
People and planet

Responsible mining and sustainability

Minerals are important to modern life, but extracting them can affect land, water, biodiversity, workers and communities. Good practice aims to prevent harm, reduce impacts and share benefits fairly.

Water protection

Monitor water quality, manage mine drainage and prevent harmful substances from reaching rivers and groundwater.

Worker safety

Use risk assessments, training, protective equipment, safe machinery and emergency plans.

Land restoration

Plan for rehabilitation, erosion control, safe tailings storage and long-term site monitoring.

Community partnership

Engage communities transparently and consider livelihoods, land rights, health and local development.

Ethical supply chains

Improve traceability and due diligence so buyers understand where minerals come from and how they were produced.

Circular economy

Design products to last, repair and reuse them, and recover valuable materials through recycling.
